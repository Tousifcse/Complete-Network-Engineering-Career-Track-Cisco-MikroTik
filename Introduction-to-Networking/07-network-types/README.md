# 07. Types of Networks

Networks are classified based on their **geographical size/coverage**.

## Types

| Type | Full Form | Range/Coverage | Example |
|------|-----------|-----------------|---------|
| **PAN** | Personal Area Network | A few meters (around one person) | Bluetooth headset connected to phone |
| **LAN** | Local Area Network | A single building/campus | Office network, home Wi-Fi |
| **MAN** | Metropolitan Area Network | A city | Cable TV network, city-wide Wi-Fi |
| **WAN** | Wide Area Network | Country/Global | The Internet |
| **CAN** | Campus Area Network | A university/college campus | University network connecting multiple buildings |

## Comparison Table

| Feature | LAN | MAN | WAN |
|---------|-----|-----|-----|
| Coverage | Small (building/campus) | Medium (city) | Large (country/world) |
| Speed | High | Moderate | Comparatively lower |
| Ownership | Single organization | Multiple organizations | Multiple organizations/ISPs |
| Cost | Low | Medium | High |
| Example | Office/School network | City surveillance network | Internet |

## Quick Notes
- **LAN** is the fastest and cheapest to set up since it covers a small area.
- **WAN** connects multiple LANs and MANs together — the Internet is the largest WAN.
- **MAN** typically covers a city and is often used by ISPs and government (e.g., city traffic camera networks).
